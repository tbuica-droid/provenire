The problem

A diligence pass is days of reading to find the few things that matter.

And the part everyone used to pay for — reading the data room and pulling the facts out — is becoming a built-in feature of the data room itself.

Extraction used to be the work. It is becoming a checkbox the buyer already pays for.

Now standard

VDRs ship their own AI copilots

Virtual data rooms now include built-in AI assistants that summarize and extract across the documents — bundled into the subscription the buyer already holds.

Emerging

Data rooms are opening up

As VDRs expose programmatic interfaces, any AI assistant can plug straight into the data room to summarize and extract, further commoditizing retrieval.

The result

"We cite sources" is now free

"We read your data room and cite the source" is no longer a product. It's a feature of the platform the deal already runs on.

Every tool can read your data room now. That was never the hard part.

The hard part is judging which findings are material — which clause actually threatens the deal, and what the upside is worth in dollars.

How it works

Ingest the data room. Get ranked, cited findings — and a draft IC memo.

One engine — finding, benchmark, quantified impact, citation — applied from the first read to the committee memo. The receipts travel with every number.

  1. Step 1

    Ingest the data room

    Point Provenire at the full deal corpus — financials, contracts, the cap table, customer data. Connect securely or upload.

    • Financial statements and working papers
    • Contracts, cap table, customer and HR data
    • Secure connection or direct upload
  2. Step 2

    Benchmark and score

    Provenire scores each finding for materiality against comparable deals — and benchmarks the target's org and economics against peers.

    • Span of control and management layers
    • Headcount-to-revenue and cost-per-FTE
    • Scored against comparable-deal medians
  3. Step 3

    Get decision-ready output

    A ranked findings list — risks and quantified cost-out — each cited to document, page, and clause, plus a first-draft IC memo.

    • Ranked by materiality, not volume
    • Every finding cited to its source
    • First-draft Investment Committee memo

Benefits

Materiality, quantified opportunity, and a citation on every line.

A ranked list, not a wall of flags.

Extraction is now table stakes; the VDR does it too. Provenire scores each finding against comparable deals and surfaces the few material enough to change the price or the structure — so the team reads "this is the one that matters," not 400 highlighted clauses.

Findings include the upside, in dollars.

Most tools only flag risk. Every claim cites the exact source.

Boundaries

What Provenire doesn't do.

The boundary is part of the product. Provenire sharpens the judgment behind the decision; it does not take the actions that require a signature, a mandate, or a model of record.

  • Build the DCF or LBO model
  • Draft the CIM or teaser
  • Author or negotiate the LOI or SPA
  • Make regulatory filings
  • Run financing or closing
  • Execute post-merger integration

Provenire proposes the number and shows its work. A human signs it.

Questions deal teams ask

The questions a skeptic asks first.

Your VDR answers "what's in here?" Provenire answers "what's material, and what's it worth?" A VDR can find every change-of-control clause; it can't tell you which one actually threatens the deal — it has no benchmark of comparable deals — and it never sees org structure to quantify cost-out. Extraction is the commodity layer the VDR owns; materiality and org benchmarking are the judgment layer it structurally can't reach. We sit on top of the data room, not against it.

Security & trust

What is built, and what is not.

Provenire is a working prototype, so the honest version matters more than the reassuring one. Below is what the code does today, followed by what would have to be built before real deal data goes near it.

Per-deal data separation

Documents, chunks, and findings are scoped to their own deal in the schema, and retrieval only ever reads the chunks belonging to the deal being analyzed.

Runs locally; nothing is retained remotely

Documents are parsed and stored on your own machine. The only data leaving it is the retrieved chunks sent to the model API for a given analysis call.

Not yet built: production controls

Encryption at rest, authentication, access control, and audit logging are not implemented. They would be required, along with a formal compliance program, before any real client data touches this. No badge is claimed that has not been earned.

A full audit trail on every finding

Each output carries a per-finding citation — document, page, and clause — so every claim traces back to your own documents. Auditability by construction.

Stated precisely, without embellishment: Provenire is a local prototype. Data stays on the machine running it, and the repository documents exactly which production security controls are absent. Treat it as a demo, not as a system of record for live deals.
